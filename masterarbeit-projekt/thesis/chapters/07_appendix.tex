\section{Fragen von Experiment 0}

\textbf{Basic Understanding (Floor, low discriminatory power (making different competency levels visible))}

1. What steps does an exploratory data analysis include, and in what order are they typically performed?

2. What is the IQR method, and what is it used for in outlier detection?

3. What do the mean, median, and mode each describe, and how do they differ?

4. What is the difference between univariate, bivariate, and multivariate analysis?

\textbf{Application and Evaluation (medium discriminatory power)}

5. When is the median preferable to the mean as a measure of central tendency, and why?

6. When is a boxplot preferable to a histogram, and when is the reverse true?

7. Which summary statistics and visualizations are suitable for a categorical variable, and which are suitable for a metric variable?

8. How can one determine in an exploratory analysis whether a variable should be transformed (e.g., using a log transformation)?

\textbf{Critical Understanding of Methods (high discriminatory power)}

9. What does a correlation coefficient not indicate, and what common misconceptions can arise when interpreting it?

10. Why can simply removing outliers be problematic, and what should the decision depend on?

11. A Pearson correlation coefficient close to zero is calculated. Why does this not necessarily mean that no relationship exists?

12. What risks arise when too many hypotheses are tested on the same data during the exploratory phase?

13. Why is exploratory data analysis not a purely automated process, and what role does the judgment of the analyst play?

14. To what extent can the choice of visualization (e.g., axis scaling, bin width) influence the interpretation of the data?

15. What does the skewness of a distribution mean, and what consequences does a highly skewed distribution have for the choice of summary statistics?
